Hvordan er det målt?

The Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S), operated by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF), collects temperature data by combining billions of physical observations from air, land, sea, and space using a method called climate reanalysis.

Observational Data Inputs:

In Situ Surface Stations: Direct air temperature readings measured at 2 meters above ground level across thousands of weather stations managed by national meteorological agencies worldwide.

Marine Sensors: Surface air, sea-surface temperature (SST), and sea-ice measurements collected from ships, drifting and moored buoys, and autonomous ocean floats.

Satellite Remote Sensing: Infrared and microwave radiances captured by polar-orbiting and geostationary satellites, which are mathematically converted into ground and atmospheric temperature readings.

Atmospheric Profiling: Temperature soundings from radiosonde weather balloons and automated sensor networks installed on commercial aircraft.
